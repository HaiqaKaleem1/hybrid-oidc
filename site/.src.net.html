05 The network — full LAN, real computers, every attack

A network of real computers connected through visible LAN gateways (switches and routers). Pick any sender and any receiver, press Send a sealed message, and watch the HMK-1 record travel across the wires through the gateways. Then open the attack drawer — there are fourteen attacks including bit-flip, tag forgery, single-half guess, double-half guess, replay, replay-old-nonce, eavesdrop, MITM reroute, keystream reuse, nonce reuse, chopped/lengthened ct, tag truncation, timing side-channel probe, and classical-only/PQ-only downgrade. Every attack fails for the same reason the construction proves it must.

How to read this: every sealed record is ct = mk ⊕ HMAC(kek, LAB_KS‖nonce) plus tag = HMAC(kmac, LAB_TAG‖nonce‖ct‖aad), with K_f = ss_c·ss_p mod p and u = M·(ss_c‖ss_p). The receiver rejects any record whose tag does not verify — and the tag cannot be forged without K_f, which needs both KEM halves. Tampering the ct, forging the tag, guessing one or both halves, replaying, reusing keystream, truncating ct or tag, side-channel probing, or downgrading to one half — all rejected by the same construction.
